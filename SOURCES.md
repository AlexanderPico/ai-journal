# Research methodology and provenance

## Dating rules

The journal was reconstructed from multiple private source classes. Public entries use this evidence order:

1. Explicit dated content and Git history
2. Original artifact creation metadata when provenance is reliable
3. Modification history when stronger evidence is unavailable
4. Operator recollection, clearly labeled as approximate

Copied, extracted, migrated, or restored files can retain misleading creation metadata. Implausible timestamps are excluded rather than treated as chronology.

## Private source classes

Detailed provenance remains outside the public repository. The private editorial record may include:

- project and repository history
- local agent-runtime configuration and operational notes
- scheduled-task history
- conversation/session history
- knowledge-system and learning-system artifacts
- migration, review, and retirement plans

The public dataset intentionally excludes filesystem paths, session identifiers, private commit identifiers, account details, security findings, and unpublished project names.

## Known gaps

- Early assistant and harness exploration is supported partly by recollection rather than one authoritative surviving artifact.
- Some early automation history may have been deleted, migrated, or remained in cloud-only systems.
- Naming, installation, and retirement transitions happened over intervals rather than at one atomic timestamp.
- Confidence labels are editorial assessments of chronology, not independent verification.

## Interpretation boundary

The journal's “continual learning” stage means maintaining durable records, incorporating judgments and outcomes, improving retrieval and policies, and testing behavior over time. It does not claim autonomous model-weight training or reliable recursive self-improvement.
